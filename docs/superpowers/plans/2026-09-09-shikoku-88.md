# Shikoku 88 Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Replace `routes/shikoku-88` — ten stages that do not chain — with four dōjō sections carrying about 45 mechanically-derived day stages, under the corpus's first `legs` pilgrimage and its first `circular` one.

**Architecture:** Four sections (`shikoku-88-awa`, `shikoku-88-tosa`, `shikoku-88-iyo`, `shikoku-88-sanuki`) declaring a `shikoku-88` pilgrimage of `kind: legs` with `circular: true`. Each section's walked line is built from pinned henro-trail relations only; day stages are cut mechanically from that line by a stated rule, never lifted from a guidebook.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript under `tsx`, `node:test` + `node:assert/strict`, Ajv against `schema/*.json`, no runtime dependencies.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-pilgrimages-and-sections-design.md`, especially §4.2 (chaining and the circular close), §4.3 (relation-less routes), §5.3 (this PR).

## What the measurements already establish

**Corrected by Task 1, which re-fetched and re-measured.** This plan first said four relations stop short of the temple they are named for — 3→4 by 431 m, 10→11 by 507 m, 19→20 by 2,839 m, 22→23 by 9,187 m — and called that "the only real defect". **Not one of them is short.** Those figures came from reading Overpass **member order as walking order**: taking the last member way's last point as where the relation ends. That point really does sit 9,187 m from Temple 23, which is why the number reproduces — but it is a way in the *middle* of relation `13692105`, not its terminus. Measured terminal to terminal, **all 88 joins are 0 m**: consecutive relations share the join node by exact coordinate identity, which is the connectivity `buildWayGraph` requires. The same misreading produced this plan's "median 34 m, p90 81 m" join gaps. **The April cache measures identically**, so nothing was fixed upstream — the measurement was wrong when it was taken.

Everything below is `.superpowers/sdd/shikoku-osm-research.md`, measured 2026-09-09 against a fresh Overpass fetch and `routes/shikoku-88/waypoints.geojson`, cross-measured against the 2026-04-09 cache to tell "OSM changed" from "we measured it wrong". **That note is the authority. Where it and this section disagree, the note wins.**

**The spec's premise about the OSM data is wrong, in our favour.** §4.3 says "an Overpass name query pulls in every spur and variant; the 4,020 km Shikoku line came from 89 relations", implying the 89 are junk to be filtered. They are not. **They are the trail itself, segmented temple-to-temple**, each named `四国遍路 N番札所X~M番札所Y`, all tagged `route=hiking`:

- **88 of them form the complete sequential circuit** — 1→2, 2→3, … 87→88, and `88番札所大窪寺~1番札所霊山寺`, the closing return to Temple 1. **No link is missing.**
- **One relation is an alternative**: `13669296`, `16番札所観音寺~18番札所恩山寺`, 19.08 km, which **skips Temple 17** — its nearest approach to Ido-ji is 1,295 m, against 48 m to T16 and 8 m to T18. It is not part of the circuit.
- **All 88 temples are already waypoints** — `templeNumber` 1–88 complete, typed `sacred_site`, carrying coordinates, `stageIndex` and `kmFromStart`.

**Every relation reaches both its named temples.** The four this plan once called short end **83 m** (3→4), **46 m** (10→11), **62 m** (19→20) and **38 m** (22→23) from their to-temple. Temple waypoints are stored to three decimal places — 80–110 m at this latitude — so a residual of tens of metres is coordinate rounding, not missing trail.

**Two lengths, and Task 3 compares against the second.** The 88 relations' member ways sum to **1,145.9 km**, against the declared 1,200 — a 4.5% gap of exactly the kind a measured line shows against a published figure, and nothing like 4,020 km. But `mainLine` takes the shortest connected path between anchors and leaves the spurs out, so **the walked line is 1,140.9 km**. That is the figure a built line is checked against.

| section | relations | walked line | worst anchor snap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shikoku-88-awa` (T1→T23) | 22 | 154.5 km | T4, 83 m |
| `shikoku-88-tosa` (T23→T39) | 16 | 418.5 km | T31, 72 m |
| `shikoku-88-iyo` (T39→T65) | 26 | 365.7 km | T49, 105 m |
| `shikoku-88-sanuki` (T65→T88→T1) | 24 | 202.2 km | T75, 102 m |

Against `SNAP_METERS` 500 (`scripts/ways/geo.ts:23`) nothing is within a factor of four of the limit, and the boundary temples are the best-anchored points on the circuit: **Temple 23 snaps at 6 m**, meeting §4.2's requirement that Awa's last stage end at the boundary temple itself with 494 m to spare.

**`validate` passes at HEAD**, with four warnings, all Camino Norte `offLineMeters` declarations. There is no Shikoku chain failure today: `shikoku-88` carries no `pilgrimage` block, and `validateSectionChain` chains only `legs` sections, so the check never reaches it. There is no Shikoku failure to fix here — there is a passing state to keep, and this PR is what first arms the chain against it.

**`refuseIncompleteLine` does not catch a missing terminal relation.** Both gates were broken deliberately on Awa:

```
drop=none      missing 0  lineKm 154.5  worstSnap    83 m
drop=22->23    missing 0  lineKm 132.9  worstSnap 13454 m
drop=11->12    missing 1  lineKm 151.2  worstSnap    83 m
```

Withholding the terminal relation leaves connectivity clean and merely shortens the line by 21.6 km; only the anchor snap catches it, at 13,454 m. Withholding a middle one is the reverse. **Task 3 reads both, never the connectivity gate alone.**

**So the day cut is comfortable in total and emphatically not leg by leg.** 1,140.9 km at the spec's 25–30 km gives 38–46 days, and at 26 km gives 43.9 — the spec's "about 45" survives. But **only 4 of the 88 temple-to-temple legs fall inside 25–30 km**, and **ten legs exceed 30 km carrying 552.1 km, 48% of the circuit**, the worst T37→T38 at 82.71 km. Across half this route there is no temple to end a day at, which is why Task 4's rule is no longer the one §5.3 words.

## Decisions already made

- **Exclude the 16→18 alternative.** The pilgrimage visits all 88 temples in order; a variant skipping Temple 17 is not the route. Record it in `osm.note` so the next reader does not rediscover it as a missing relation.
- **Boundary relations belong to the section that walks them.** Awa owns 1→2 … 22→23 and **ends at Temple 23**. Tosa owns 23→24 … 38→39 and **begins at Temple 23**. Iyo owns 39→40 … 64→65. Sanuki owns 65→66 … 87→88 **and 88→1**, the closing return. This is what §4.2's "a boundary stage ends and starts at the boundary place itself" requires.
- **`overview.topology` becomes `linear` on every section.** Each dōjō is individually linear — Awa runs Temple 1 to 23 and does not return. The circuit is claimed by the pilgrimage block's `circular: true`, which every section repeats. §4.2 is explicit that no honest section-level topology could arm the circuit check.
- **`shikoku-88` stops being a route id and becomes a pilgrimage id**, exactly as `kumano-kodo` did in 1.8.0. This is the release's one consumer-breaking change.

## Task 4: About forty-five days, cut mechanically

The heart of the PR, and the part that must be **reproducible rather than authored**.

**Files:** `routes/shikoku-88-*/stages.json`

- [ ] **Step 1: Apply the day rule, as the owner ruled it**

**Corrected after Task 1 measured the leg distribution.** This plan first said to implement §5.3 literally — *the next temple in sequence that puts the day between 25 and 30 km along the walked line, or at that temple's town where the temple has no lodging* — and left the no-temple-in-range case as a tie-break to be stated. The measurements do not leave room for that reading. **Only 4 of the 88 legs fall inside 25–30 km.** **Ten legs exceed 30 km and carry 552.1 km — 48% of the circuit** — the worst Temple 37 → Temple 38 at 82.71 km. Awa is clean (longest leg 22.69 km, so every Awa day can end at a temple), but Tosa has 5 such legs, Iyo 4 and Sanuki 1. Across half the route there is simply no temple to end a day at, and no tie-break over temples can invent one.

The repo owner has decided the rule. Implement it as worded:

> A day ends at a temple whenever one falls between 25 and 30 km along the walked line. Where no temple does, it ends at the **nearest named `accommodation` or `town` waypoint on the line** instead.

It stays fully mechanical and re-runnable. Both arms read the section's own `waypoints.geojson` against its `route.main.geojson` and nothing is chosen by hand. "Nearest" is nearest along the line to where the day would otherwise have ended, not nearest in space; "named" excludes the `Unnamed` placeholder `extractName` hands back, because a walker cannot be told to stop at one — `routes/shikoku-88/waypoints.geojson` carries 510 of them across its 2,980 features.

**Check what the fallback has to work with before cutting.** That same file holds **124 `accommodation` waypoints, 122 of them named, and zero `town`** — the `place=city|town|village` clause reached `buildPoiQuery` after this route was last enriched. If the fallback cannot find a candidate for a stage, say so and report which; do not widen the rule to reach one.

**Step past the two legs shorter than half a kilometre.** T68→T69 is 0.11 km and T72→T73 is 0.46 km — temples sharing a compound. A greedy cut must carry on to the next candidate rather than emit a stage of that length.

- [ ] **Step 2: Boundary stages end at the boundary temple, never its town**

§4.2 and §5.3 both say this. Awa's last stage ends at Temple 23; Tosa's first begins there. **The fallback arm does not apply at a boundary** — a boundary day ends at the temple whatever its distance comes to. Sanuki's final stage runs Ōkubo-ji (88) → Ryōzen-ji (1).

- [ ] **Step 3: Record the derivation in `provenance.sources`**

It must record **both halves — the rule and why its second arm exists**: that the spec's temple-spacing assumption holds for 4 of the 88 legs, and that ten legs carrying 48% of the circuit have no temple in range. A reader given only "25–30 km to a temple" cannot account for the stages that end somewhere else, and would read them as authored.

## What this plan does not do

- **It does not invent geometry.** If a relation gap cannot be closed, the section ships `metadataOnly` and waits (§4.3). Task 1 decides this before any work depends on it.
- **It does not lift a guidebook's stage pacing.** The day cut is mechanical and its rule is recorded, because a pacing this repo could neither cite nor relicense is not shippable.
- **It does not add the 16→18 alternative.** The route visits all 88 temples in order.
- **It does not carry Shikoku's legends into stage text.** §6 admits only what the section's own data holds, and this is the route where that restraint costs the most and matters the most.
