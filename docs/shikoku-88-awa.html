Awa (Temples 1-23)

Ryōzen-ji (Temple 1) to Yakuō-ji (Temple 23) · the first of the four dōjō

Part of the Shikoku 88 Temple Pilgrimage.

Awa is the first of the four dōjō — the four old provinces the Shikoku circuit crosses, one section each. It runs 154.5 km from Ryōzen-ji, Temple 1, where the circuit begins, to Yakuō-ji, Temple 23, where Tosa takes it up from the same point, and 23 of the pilgrimage's 88 temples stand on it. That 154.5 km is this section's own walked line, measured through the 22 henro trail relations it pins in OpenStreetMap; the four dōjō together measure 1,140.9 km against the pilgrimage's traditional ~1,200 km figure.

The five day stages below were cut from that line by a rule rather than copied from a guidebook, so anyone with the files can re-run them and get the same days.

Elevation

This section publishes no elevation profile, because it holds no elevation to draw one from. The walked line is two-dimensional — not one of route.main.geojson's 7,054 vertices carries a third coordinate — and no stage in stages.json declares an ascent or a descent. Read that as unmeasured rather than as level ground: the henro trail climbs to mountain temples, and this dataset simply does not yet hold the heights along it. Nothing here should be read as a claim that the walking is flat.

What the files do hold are point heights on temples, where OpenStreetMap tagged one. Eighteen of the 88 temple waypoints carry one — three in Awa, three in Tosa, five in Iyo and seven in Sanuki. Awa's three are Shōzan-ji (Temple 12) at 700 m, Kakurin-ji (Temple 20) at 470 m and Tairyū-ji (Temple 21) at 600 m. A height at a gate is not a profile between gates, and nothing here interpolates one.

A day ends at a temple whenever one falls between 25 and 30 km along the walked line; where none does, it ends at the nearest named accommodation or town waypoint on the line instead, and a day that comes within 30 km of the section's own end anchor ends there whatever its length. Three of Awa's five days end at a temple and two at a lodging. Every distance is the length of route.main.geojson between that day's own two anchors, and every anchor carries a note in stages.json saying which arm of the rule chose it; the whole rule, its tie-breaks and the readings it refuses are written out in metadata.json's provenance.

Stage 4 runs 52.9 km, the longest day in this section, and it is long because of an absence rather than a distance: OpenStreetMap records no named accommodation and no town on the walked line between Ido-ji and where the day ends, so the fallback had nowhere nearer to stop. The stage carries that as a warning in stages.json. No maximum refuses a day that long, because refusing would leave the stretch with no day-end at all.

Interior Journey

The narrative and reflection prompt for each stage below are editorial writing, grounded only in what the stage contains — its temples, shrines, towns, spacing and distance. All 5 have been read against that evidence and cleared by a reviewer who did not write them. See docs/review/shikoku-88.md for the checks stage by stage, and docs/data-sources.md for what is, and isn't, independently sourced.

Stage 1 Ryōzen-ji (Temple 1) → Kirihata-ji (Temple 10) 28.3 km · Ten temples in twenty-eight kilometres

Ten of Awa's twenty-three temples stand on this one day. Ryōzen-ji opens it, Gokuraku-ji comes 1.3 km on, and Konsen-ji, Dainichi-ji, Jizō-ji, Anraku-ji, Jūraku-ji, Kumadani-ji and Hōrin-ji follow before Kirihata-ji closes it at 28.3 km. The widest gap between any two of them is 5.3 km. What else the day passes is near at hand — the ruins of the former Bandō POW camp inside the first kilometre, the town of Itano at 4.8 km. Ten arrivals in the first 28.3 km of a 154.5 km section: whatever this day asks of you, it is not patience between temples.

Ten arrivals in one day, the widest gap between any two of them 5.3 km — how do you meet the tenth as freshly as the first?

Stage 2 Kirihata-ji (Temple 10) → Sudachi-an 24.3 km · From ten temples to two

Yesterday held ten temples; today holds two. Fujii-dera comes 9.7 km on from Kirihata-ji and Shōzan-ji 11.6 km after that — the two widest gaps between temples Awa has offered so far, and both are wider than any gap in yesterday's ten. Between them the data records three more sacred places, Chōdo-an, ishidou-gongen and Ryūsui-an, inside 2.9 km. No temple falls where a day of this length would end, so this one does not end at a gate: it stops 3.1 km past Shōzan-ji at Sudachi-an, a guesthouse.

The day ends at a guesthouse because no temple fell where the day did — does the walking change when there is nothing at the end of it to arrive at?

Stage 3 Sudachi-an → Ido-ji (Temple 17) 26.9 km · Five temples in the last eight kilometres

The day walks 18.8 km before it reaches a temple. Shōzan-ji is 21.8 km behind by then, the second-widest step between two temples in this section, and the day's first is Dainichi-ji at 71.3 km — the second temple of that name in Awa, after Temple 4 on the opening day. Then the spacing collapses: Jōraku-ji, Awa Kokubun-ji, Kannon-ji and Ido-ji all fall inside the 8.1 km after it, and Jōraku-ji to Awa Kokubun-ji is 1.1 km, the shortest step between two temples anywhere in Awa. Hie Shrine, Hachiman Shrine and Jigen-ji stand in that same run. One day carries both shapes the section has.

18.8 km to the day's first temple, then four more inside the 8.1 km after it — which of the two is the walking you came for?

Stage 4 Ido-ji (Temple 17) → GuestHouse & Cafe Green House 52.9 km · Fifty-three kilometres between beds

Awa's longest day, 52.9 km, and it is long because of an absence rather than a distance: between Ido-ji and this day's end the data holds no accommodation and no town anywhere on the walked line, so the cut had nowhere nearer to stop. The stretch itself is the busiest in the section — eighty-nine of Awa's two hundred and three recorded places stand on it, more than on any other day, twenty-two of them places to eat. Five temples come with it: Onzan-ji 18.1 km in, then Tatsue-ji, Kakurin-ji, Tairyū-ji and Byōdō-ji. Past Byōdō-ji the first bed the record holds is 0.6 km on, at the GuestHouse & Cafe Green House, and the day ends there.

A day with everything on it but a bed — is it long because the way is, or because of where the record let it end?

Warning: 52.9 km, the longest of Awa's five days. OpenStreetMap records no named accommodation or town on the walked line between Ido-ji and this day's end, so the rule had nowhere nearer to stop. Carry water and food for the whole distance, or break the day at a place off the line.

Stage 5 GuestHouse & Cafe Green House → Yakuō-ji (Temple 23) 22.1 km · One temple, at the end of it

One temple stands on this day and it is the last thing on it. Byōdō-ji to Yakuō-ji is 22.7 km, the widest step between two temples in Awa, and the day walks all but 0.6 km of it. The first 11.2 km carry no recorded place at all — the longest stretch in the section with nothing marked on it. Then three stations come one after another, Yuki, Tainohama and Kiki, and inside the last 1.5 km the day gathers what the rest of it withheld: Hiwasa Hachiman Shrine, the town of Minami, a clinic, a restaurant and two places to stay. Yakuō-ji closes Awa at 154.5 km, and Tosa begins from the same point.

Twenty-two kilometres to one gate, and the section ends at it — what do you want to be carrying when you get there?

index.json records this section as sparse: false, at 9.6 places per stage. That bar belongs to a section, not to a day: a section clears it when at least half its stages carry a place beyond their own start and end, and all five of Awa's do. The 9.6 is the mean of those beyond-the-ends counts — 13, 7, 7, 17 and 4 — so it is not the number of places you would pass in a day.

Seven waypoints in this file never reach a stage. The corridor that admitted them is measured against route.geojson, which carries every member way of the 22 relations and runs 349.7 km against the walked line's 154.5; build-ways then measures against the walked line and drops anything more than 300 m from it. Geometry

The walked line is cut from 22 OpenStreetMap relations, one per temple-to-temple leg from Temple 1 to Temple 23, in walking order. The Overpass query is pilgrimage-wide and returns all 89 四国遍路 relations; each section pins the slice of that result it walks, and a boundary relation belongs to the section that walks it — so 22番札所~23番札所 is here, and Awa ends at Temple 23 where Tosa begins.

One relation is excluded on purpose rather than missing. 13669296, 四国遍路 16番札所観音寺~18番札所恩山寺, is an alternative that skips Temple 17: it passes no closer than 1,295 m to Ido-ji, against 48 m to Temple 16 and 8 m to Temple 18. The circuit's own path, 16→17 then 17→18, runs 21.00 km against the alternative's 19.08, so the shortcut saves 1.92 km by leaving a temple out. The pilgrimage visits all 88 in order.

route.geojson holds all of those relations' member ways, 7,660 points and 349.7 km of them, including the spurs and side paths a walker does not follow. route.main.geojson is the shortest connected path through the same graph — 7,054 points, 154.5 km — and it is the line every stage, every stage distance and every waypoint position on this page is measured along.

Tradition

Primary faith: Shingon Buddhism

The circuit traces the path of the monk Kūkai (774–835), Kōbō Daishi, who founded Shingon Buddhism in Japan and practised asceticism across Shikoku before founding the monastic centre on Mount Kōya. The 88-temple circuit was formalised during the Edo period.

The pilgrimage maps a stage of spiritual progression onto each of Shikoku's four provinces, and the one this section's metadata.json records for Awa is awakening.

Credential: the nōkyōchō (納経帳), a calligraphy book bought at Temple 1 and carried across all four dōjō. At each temple the monks write the temple name, its principal deity and its number, and apply three red stamps. A completed book earns the kechigan-shō, the completion certificate — which is awarded for the circuit, not for any one section of it.
