Camino Inglés (English Way)

Ferrol to Santiago · the shortest of the major Caminos

The shortest of the major Caminos to Santiago de Compostela, walked since the medieval period by English, Irish, Scandinavian, and Flemish pilgrims who arrived by sea at the Galician ports of Ferrol and A Coruña. The standard route from Ferrol covers approximately 112 km along the Ría de Ferrol coast, through the medieval Gothic town of Betanzos, over the Bruma highlands, and down into Santiago. Modern revival began in earnest in the 2000s; in 2025 over 30,000 pilgrims walked it.

Tradition & Credential

Saint: Saint James the Great

The Camino Inglés takes its name from medieval pilgrims who arrived by sea at the Galician ports of Ferrol and A Coruña, primarily from England, Ireland, Scandinavia, and Flanders. The route's peak use occurred during the Hundred Years' War (1337–1453), when English pilgrims and traders accessed Galician ports under royal license. The two historic starting cities — A Coruña (closer, ~75 km) and Ferrol (further, ~112 km) — diverge but converge at Hospital de Bruma. Modern pilgrims overwhelmingly start from Ferrol because A Coruña falls below the 100 km Compostela minimum walking distance.

UNESCO status: Not independently UNESCO-inscribed.

Credential: Standard Pilgrim Credential (Credencial del Peregrino), stamped at least twice daily. From Ferrol it is ~112 km to Santiago, comfortably above the 100 km Compostela minimum; from A Coruña it is only ~75 km, which does not qualify on its own. Since December 2016, the Celtic Camino program lets A Coruña pilgrims qualify for the Compostela by walking at least 25 km on a certified Jacobean route in their home country before continuing from A Coruña. Credentials are issued at the Concatedral de San Xulián de Ferrol, the tourism office at the Curuxeiras pier, and the Confraternity of Saint James (UK).
