Camino del Norte (Northern Way)

Irún to Santiago · the coastal Camino

The coastal Camino de Santiago along the Bay of Biscay from Irún on the French border, through the Basque Country, Cantabria, Asturias, and into Galicia where it joins the Camino Francés at Arzúa for the final approach to Santiago de Compostela. Approximately 784 km in 34 native stages plus a 39 km shared run on the Francés to Santiago. Among the oldest Caminos — walked when inland Iberia was under Moorish control and the Atlantic coast remained the safer Christian corridor. The longest non-Francés Camino and the only major Camino that stays within sight of the sea for most of its length. UNESCO inscribed in 2015 as part of the Routes of Northern Spain extension.

Stage 33, Baamonde → Sobrado dos Monxes, is the longest single day at 39.8 km. There is no single dramatic mountain pass like the Primitivo's Puerto del Palo; the Norte's difficulty instead comes from constant short climbs and descents across coastal headlands, accumulating more total elevation gain than any other Camino in this dataset.

Tradition & Credential

Saint: Saint James the Great

The Camino del Norte is among the earliest Jacobean pilgrimage routes, emerging in the 9th century shortly after the discovery of the apostle's tomb at Iria Flavia. It follows segments of the old Roman Via Agrippa along the Cantabrian coast. During the 9th and 10th centuries, when much of the Iberian interior was under Moorish control, the coastal north remained under Christian rule — centred on the Kingdom of Asturias — making it the safer route for pilgrims, especially nobility arriving by ship at Cantabrian ports. With the southward Reconquista and the royal promotion of the Camino Francés from the late 11th century, the Norte declined in favour of the better-infrastructured French Way, though it remained in continuous use and was formally revived in the 20th century.

UNESCO status: World Heritage Site, inscribed 2015, as part of the “Routes of Santiago de Compostela: Camino Francés and Routes of Northern Spain” extension, which added the Camino del Norte, Primitivo, Lebaniego, and Vasco del Interior routes to the original 1993 Camino Francés inscription.

Credential: Standard Pilgrim Credential (Credencial del Peregrino), stamped at least twice daily. From Sobrado dos Monxes it is ~100 km to Santiago — the canonical last-100-km starting point — though many pilgrims walking just the minimum distance start from Vilalba (~120 km) or Baamonde (~105 km). Credentials are issued at the Parroquia de Nuestra Señora del Juncal in Irún, the Basílica de Begoña in Bilbao, and the Monasterio de Sobrado dos Monxes.
