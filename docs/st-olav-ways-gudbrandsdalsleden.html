Gudbrandsdalsleden

Oslo to Nidaros Cathedral · the inland St. Olav Way through Gudbrandsdalen

Part of the St. Olav Ways.

The inland pilgrim way from Oslo to Nidaros Cathedral in Trondheim: 643 km from the ruins of St Mary's Church in Oslo's medieval town, over Hadeland, past Gjøvik and Lillehammer, through Gudbrandsdalen and across Dovrefjell, then down through Oppdal and Skaun to the cathedral where St Olav is buried. 24 days here, the high point 1,310 m on Dovrefjell, with about 11,960 m of ascent against 11,880 m of descent.

Elevation Profile

Ascent, descent and every high and low point on this page are sampled from EU-DEM v1.1, the Copernicus 25 m digital elevation model, at each of the walked line's 31,116 vertices, with a 20 m threshold so the model's own noise is not counted as climbing. Not SRTM 30 m, which the rest of this dataset uses, because SRTM has no data north of 60° N and this route runs from 59.9° to 63.4° N.

The days are cut by a rule rather than taken from a guidebook: each ends at a named place where OpenStreetMap maps a bed within reach of the line, aiming for 20–30 km, and the “How the day ends” column names that bed. 18 of the 24 days fall inside that band. Where OpenStreetMap maps no lodging the days run long — 49.6 km, 34.2 km, 34.6 km, 34.6 km — and each of those stages carries a warning in stages.json. Hours are Naismith's rule at two paces with no roughness factor; overview.hoursNote says why.

Interior Journey

The narrative and reflection prompt for each stage below are editorial writing, grounded only in what the stage contains — the churches, villages and lodging OpenStreetMap maps along it, and its measured distance and climb. All 24 were reviewed by someone who did not write them. See docs/review/st-olav-ways-gudbrandsdalsleden.md for what each was written from and that review, and docs/data-sources.md for what is, and isn't, independently sourced.

Stage 1 Mariakirken → Sæteren gård 23.4 km · Out through the old town

The way begins among the ruins of St Mary's Church in Oslo's medieval town and leaves the city on foot. It passes close to Gamle Aker kirke about four and a half kilometres in, then a run of city churches and suburbs, and passes Haslum some twenty kilometres out. The day ends at Sæteren gård, 23.4 km from the start, having climbed 300 m and come down 120 m, with its high point at 210 m.

You leave from the ruins of a church to walk to a cathedral. What are you carrying out of the city with you?

Stage 2 Sæteren gård → Sundvollen 24.9 km · A day without villages

From Sæteren gård to Sundvollen OpenStreetMap records no village and no church within reach of the line: 24.9 km between one bed and the next. The day rises to 500 m and drops to 70 m before Sundvollen, 460 m of climbing and 570 m of descent in all.

With nothing to arrive at until evening, where does your attention go?

Stage 3 Sundvollen → Klekken 24.9 km · Two churches on the way

Out of Sundvollen the way passes Vik early on, then two old parish churches set close to the line: Hole kirke a little under thirteen kilometres in, and Norderhov kirke at about twenty. It ends at Klekken after 24.9 km, with 310 m of ascent and 180 m of descent.

Two churches in an afternoon. What would you stop to ask in each?

Stage 4 Klekken → Granavollen 31.3 km · To the sister churches

This is a long day, 31.3 km, because OpenStreetMap shows no bed between 20 and 30 km out of Klekken. It passes Haug kirke in its first kilometre and Jevnaker kirke at about thirteen and a half, and ends at Granavollen, where two churches, Mariakirken and Nikolaikirken, stand side by side as Søsterkirkene, the sister churches. 600 m up, 450 m down, the high point 410 m.

Two churches built side by side. Who walks beside you, even when you walk alone?

Stage 5 Granavollen → Alstad 49.6 km · The longest day

49.6 km, the longest stage of the route, and long only because OpenStreetMap maps no bed between 20 and 30 km out of Granavollen. It passes Tingelstad gamle kirke within the first three and a half kilometres, then Brandbu, and much later Kolbu kirke and Lena, before Alstad. It climbs 700 m and descends 820 m. The stage marks where the map shows a bed, not a recommended day.

When a day is longer than you would choose, what do you let go of to finish it?

Stage 6 Alstad → Gjøvik 24.4 km · Into Gjøvik

The day starts beside Hoff kirke, passes Kapp kirke and the village of Kapp, and ends in the town of Gjøvik by Gjøvik kirke: 24.4 km, 170 m of ascent, 250 m of descent.

After the longest day, an ordinary one. What does ordinary feel like now?

Stage 7 Gjøvik → Biri 22.7 km · Between towns

Out of Gjøvik the line passes Redalen about halfway and reaches Biri after 22.7 km. It climbs 520 m and descends 520 m, between a low of 130 m and a high of 360 m.

Halfway between two places, which one are you thinking about?

Stage 8 Biri → Vingnes 25.4 km · Towards Lillehammer

The day leaves Biri past Biri kirke, passes two campsites, then Vingrom and Vingrom kirke, and ends at Vingnes, across from Lillehammer: 25.4 km, 250 m up and 260 m down.

The next town is in sight before the day is over. How do you walk the last stretch?

Stage 9 Vingnes → Kruke 25.2 km · Through Lillehammer

Within two kilometres the way is in Lillehammer, past Lillehammer kirke, and then it goes on north through Fåberg. The day ends at Kruke, by Skåden Gard, 25.2 km on, after 660 m of climbing and 290 m of descent.

A town full of beds, and you walk on through it. What makes you keep going?

Stage 10 Kruke → Sletring 24.9 km · A loft for pilgrims

No church and no village lies near the line on this stage, but about twelve kilometres in it passes Pilegrimsloftet Borkerud, a hostel whose name means the pilgrims' loft. It ends at Sletring after 24.9 km, with 660 m of ascent and 860 m of descent.

Someone made a room for walkers like you. What would you leave behind for the next one?

Stage 11 Sletring → Frya 24.7 km · The stave church

About fourteen kilometres in the way passes Ringebu stavkyrkje, the stave church at Ringebu, then the village itself, and ends at Frya: 24.7 km, 390 m up, 440 m down.

A church built of wood has stood long enough to be called old. What in you has lasted that long?

Stage 12 Frya → Klomstad 29.8 km · Farms along the valley

Over 29.8 km the way passes the farm lodgings of Rudi gard and Sygard Grytting about nine kilometres in and ends at Klomstad, with 860 m of ascent and 780 m of descent.

Farms that take in walkers. What does it mean to be a guest?

Stage 13 Klomstad → Rukin 25.4 km · Past Kvam and Sjoa

Kvam comes early, then two campsites at Sjoa a little after halfway; the day ends at Rukin, near Otta Turistsenter, after 25.4 km. 720 m of climbing, 710 m of descent, the high point 580 m.

The valley narrows the choices. Which ones are you glad not to have?

Stage 14 Rukin → Rindal 25.2 km · Sel and Nord-Sel

The stage starts beside Sel kirke and passes Nord-Sel kirke at about eleven kilometres, then Dovreskogen before Rindal: 25.2 km, 580 m up and 290 m down.

Two churches with one name between them. What do you share with the people who walked here before?

Stage 15 Rindal → Fokstugu fjellstue 24.7 km · Up to Fokstugu

The way passes Dovre and Dovre kyrkje before eight kilometres, then Pilgrimsgården Busjord, and follows the old King's Road, Kongeveien over Dovre, up to Fokstugu fjellstue. The day climbs 840 m and descends 480 m over 24.7 km, its high point 1,210 m.

The road climbs out of the valley. What do you hope to see from higher up?

Stage 16 Fokstugu fjellstue → Hjerkinn fjellstue 24.5 km · Across Dovrefjell

From Fokstugu the King's Road crosses Dovrefjell to Hageseter, nearly twenty kilometres on, and then to Hjerkinn fjellstue: 24.5 km, between 900 m and 1,200 m, with 340 m of ascent and 370 m of descent.

Up here there is a great deal of sky. What feels small, and what feels large?

Stage 17 Hjerkinn fjellstue → Ryphusan 34.2 km · Past Eysteinkyrkja

A kilometre out of Hjerkinn the way passes Eysteinkyrkja, then Kongsvold Fjeldstue at about thirteen kilometres, and goes on to Ryphusan: 34.2 km, long because OpenStreetMap maps no bed between 20 and 30 km out of Hjerkinn. It climbs 1,120 m and descends 960 m.

A long day after the high ground. How do you pace yourself when you cannot see the end?

Stage 18 Ryphusan → Aungrenda 27 km · Down to Oppdal

The day passes St. Michaels kapell, then Driva, and ends at Aungrenda near Oppdal kirke, which lies about two kilometres before the end: 27 km, 230 m of ascent and 740 m of descent.

Back among houses after the mountain. What do you want to keep from up there?

Stage 19 Aungrenda → Hæverstølen 24.2 km · Nothing on the map

From Aungrenda to Hæverstølen OpenStreetMap records no church, no village and no other lodging near the line. 24.2 km, 230 m up, 260 m down, the high point 710 m.

A day the map leaves blank. What do you fill it with?

Stage 20 Hæverstølen → Voll 25 km · To Voll

The way passes Gjesteheim Havdal early and ends at Voll, by Rennebu kirke: 25 km, with 540 m of climbing and 830 m of descent.

Four days from the cathedral. What has changed in how you walk?

Stage 21 Voll → Løkken Verk 34.6 km · Ry, Meldal and Løkken Verk

Past Ry Herberge in the first seven kilometres, then Meldal and Meldal kirke, and on to Løkken Verk: 34.6 km, long because no bed is mapped between 20 and 30 km out of Voll. 380 m up and 430 m down.

A long day near the end. What are you walking towards now?

Stage 22 Løkken Verk → Hongslo 8.9 km · A short day

Only 8.9 km, from Løkken Verk to Hongslo by Gumdal Farm, because the next mapped bed near the line after it is 34.6 km further on. 140 m of climbing, 150 m of descent.

An afternoon with nowhere to be. How do you spend it?

Stage 23 Hongslo → Heggen 34.6 km · Past Skaun

The way passes Skaun kirke at about nineteen kilometres and Buvika at thirty-one, and ends at Heggen by Øysand Camping: 34.6 km, 640 m up and 810 m down.

One day left. What would you like to have finished before you arrive?

Stage 24 Heggen → Nidarosdomen 23.5 km · Into Nidaros

The last day passes Haltdalen stavkirke and Lo kirke at about nineteen kilometres, then Ilen kirke, and comes into Trondheim to end at Nidarosdomen, where St Olav is buried: 23.5 km, 320 m of ascent and 310 m of descent. Beside the cathedral, Nidaros Pilgrimsgård is mapped as a stamp office for pilgrims.

You have arrived where the way was always going. What did you come to say?

When to Walk

The national pilgrim office recommends walking between 1 June and 1 September, to avoid snow and meltwater; places to stay may be closed outside that window. Do not cross Dovrefjell before about 10 June: until then the crossing has no infrastructure in place, the bridges over the Hondyrju and Vesle Hondyrju rivers are laid out only around that date, snow can hide the waymarks, and wild reindeer are calving. Even in summer the temperature on the plateau can fall towards 0 °C, and the office suggests taking the train over Dovre when the crossing is not open.

Season, credential and pilgrim figures on this page come from the national pilgrim office, Nasjonalt pilegrimssenter, which has granted their reuse under ODbL with attribution. See metadata.json provenance.

Geometry

The walked line is cut from eight OpenStreetMap relations of the Pilegrimsleden superroute (1370274), chained south to north: 1370273 (Gudbrandsdalsleden, Oslo to Lillehammer), 5659125 (Lillehammer to Dovre), five relations of Kongeveien over Dovre over Dovrefjell and down to Skaun — 3917970, 3918309, 1671572, 5677571 and 5697677, all tagged with Wikidata's item for the pilgrim way over Dovrefjell — and 5659103 (Skaun to Nidaros). The walked line is the shortest connected path through them, held to the route by every stage anchor; it leaves out the spurs their member ways also hold.

Tradition

Primary faith: Christian

The route leads to the grave of Olav II Haraldsson, King of Norway from 1015 to 1028, who died at Stiklestad on 29 July 1030 and is buried in Nidaros Cathedral. He is venerated as a saint, and 29 July, the day of his death, is his feast, Olsok.

In the Middle Ages the Gudbrandsdalsleden was the main road to Trondheim and Nidaros Cathedral, where St Olav is buried. Norwegian authorities began restoring the old pilgrim routes in the 1990s, and the St. Olav Ways now total more than 3,800 km, of which about 2,770 km are on land; several of them belong to the association that holds their status as a Council of Europe Cultural Route. The year 2030 marks 1,000 years since St Olav's death at Stiklestad.

Credential: Pilgrims can carry a pilgrim passport (pilegrimspass) and collect stamps in it along the way, at pilgrim hostels, regional pilgrim centres and churches; where a place to stay has no stamp, the host can sign it instead. Carrying one is optional. The passport costs NOK 50 and is sold at the regional pilgrim centres and at other places along the route, among them the centres in Oslo, Granavollen and on Dovrefjell. The Olavsbrev is given to pilgrims who show a stamped passport for at least the last 100 km on foot to Nidaros Cathedral, or the last 200 km by bicycle. On this route the office suggests Berkåk to Trondheim as a walk that qualifies. Pilgrims who do not qualify can receive a pilgrim diploma instead.
