Camino de Santiago (Francés)

Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Santiago · the most-walked Camino

The most popular pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela, crossing the Pyrenees from France and traversing northern Spain for 764 km. Walked continuously since the 9th century.

Stage 1, over the Pyrenees to Roncesvalles, is the hardest single day despite its moderate overall difficulty rating — 1,419 m of ascent. The final week through Galicia (stages 26–33) is heavily trafficked, since it overlaps with the last-100-km Sarria start used by pilgrims walking only the Compostela minimum.

Tradition & Credential

Saint: Saint James the Great

Medieval pilgrimage route to the shrine of the apostle Saint James, established in the 9th century following the discovery of the apostle's remains in what is now Santiago de Compostela. One of the three great medieval Christian pilgrimages alongside Rome and Jerusalem.

UNESCO status: World Heritage Site, inscribed 1993 as “Route of Santiago de Compostela” (the Spanish portion; the French portion was separately inscribed in 1998). In 2015 the inscription was expanded and renamed “Routes of Santiago de Compostela: Camino Francés and Routes of Northern Spain,” adding the Camino del Norte, Primitivo, and other northern routes.

Credential: Pilgrim Credential (Credencial del Peregrino), a passport-like booklet stamped at churches, albergues, cafés, and other locations, at least twice daily on the qualifying distance. Walking at least 100 km (or cycling 200 km) earns the Compostela, issued for religious, spiritual, or existential motivations; a separate Certificado de Bienvenida is available for cultural or tourism motivations. Credentials are issued at the Pilgrim Welcome Office in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and by Friends of the Camino associations worldwide.
